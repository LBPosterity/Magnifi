■ Profit to Cash

Where did the profit go?

A healthy P&L doesn't always mean a healthy bank balance. Magnifi's Profit to Cash report shows you exactly how your reported profit converts — or doesn't — into actual cash, and where the difference went.

Profit to Cash is a structured reconciliation built into the Magnifi dashboard. It starts with your reported profit and works down to your closing cash position — accounting for non-cash items, capital movements, and the timing gap between invoicing and collection.

What it is

The bridge between your P&L and your bank account.

Profit to Cash in Magnifi is a structured report that starts with your reported profit and works down to your closing cash position — accounting for every item that explains why the two numbers are different. Run it for actuals, budgets, or a mix of both.

Profitable businesses run out of cash every day. A strong P&L tells you the business is generating surplus — it doesn't tell you where that surplus has gone, or whether you'll have enough to cover next month's commitments. The gap is usually some combination of non-cash accounting entries, capital movements like hire purchase repayments or asset purchases, and the timing difference between when revenue is recognised and when customers actually pay.

Magnifi calculates each of these movements automatically from your actuals and forecast, so you can see the full picture without building it yourself. The report is available as a dashboard tab and updates each time you import.

What's in it

Four sections. One complete picture.

The report works top-down — each section addresses a different reason why cash and profit diverge.

Section 01

Cash Profit

Starts with reported profit and adds back non-cash items — depreciation, accrued wages, leave provisions, and any other accounts flagged as non-cash in your chart of accounts. The result is what the business generated in cash terms, before capital transactions and timing.

Reported Profit + Non-Cash Items = Cash Profit
Section 02

Free Cash Flow

Adjusts Cash Profit for cash movements that don't flow through the P&L — loan receipts, hire purchase repayments, fixed asset purchases, director loan movements, and dividends. The result is the net cash position after all operating and capital transactions.

Cash Profit ± Other Cash Movements = Free Cash Flow
Section 03

Cash Timing Adjustments

Accounts for the gap between when revenue and costs hit the P&L and when cash actually moves. Rising debtors mean revenue has been recognised but not collected. Falling creditors mean bills have been paid ahead of when expenses were recognised.

Free Cash Flow − Timing Gap = Net Cash Surplus/(Deficiency)
Section 04

Closing Cash Position

Brings it home: Free Cash Flow adjusted for timing movements gives a Net Cash Surplus or Deficiency for the period. Add that to your opening cash balance and you have your closing position — tied back to the balance sheet.

Opening Cash ± Net Movement = Closing Cash

FAQ

Common questions

My business is profitable — why would I need this report?
Profit and cash are different things. Profit is calculated on an accrual basis — revenue is recognised when invoiced, costs when incurred, regardless of when money changes hands. Cash is what's actually in the bank. The gap between the two is usually made up of timing (debtors not yet collected, creditors not yet paid), non-cash entries like depreciation and provisions, and capital movements like loan repayments or asset purchases that don't touch the P&L at all. Profit to Cash makes that gap visible and breaks it into its components — so you know whether a cash shortfall is structural, temporary, or driven by a specific issue like slow collections.
What counts as a non-cash item?
Non-cash items are P&L entries that reduce profit without involving an actual cash outflow in that period. Depreciation is the most common example — it reduces profit each month but the cash left the business when the asset was purchased, not gradually over time. Accrued wages and annual leave provisions work the same way. In Magnifi, you flag these accounts in your chart of accounts mapping and they are added back automatically in the Cash Profit calculation. Your accountant can advise on which accounts in your specific chart of accounts should be treated as non-cash.
Can I use this report to present to a client or board?
Yes. The Profit to Cash tab is included in the live dashboard link you share from an entity or group. The recipient sees the full dashboard — including Profit to Cash — updated as of your most recent import. No login is required. You can share the entity view, the group view, or both, and revoke access at any time.
Is this the same as a formal cash flow statement?
No. Magnifi includes a separate Cash Flow report that presents operating versus non-operating cash movements in a simplified format. Profit to Cash is a management-focused view — it's designed to answer the question "where did the profit go?" in a way that's accessible to business owners, not just accountants. The two reports draw on the same underlying data but present it differently and serve different purposes.
