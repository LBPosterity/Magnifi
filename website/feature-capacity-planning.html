What it is

The fourth view that changes everything

Capacity planning in Magnifi is the process of forecasting your team's income-earning potential — from their available hours, productivity, recovery and charge-out rates — and comparing it against your projected income to reveal whether you have a surplus or a shortfall.

In a service business, people are the product. Every dollar of revenue depends on someone's hours, skill and charge-out rate. Yet most forecasting tools treat headcount as a cost — not as the engine of income.

When capacity and income are in balance, profit is maximised. Too much capacity means idle overhead; too little means burnout and unmet demand. Magnifi makes that balance visible — and keeps it connected to every other part of your forecast, so a change to any employee flows through automatically.

Common questions

Capacity planning — answered

What's the difference between Productivity % and Recovery %?
They measure different things and diagnose different problems. Productivity % is the proportion of total working hours your team spends on billable work — client-facing time rather than admin, training or downtime. Recovery % (sometimes called Efficiency %) is the proportion of that productive time that actually gets invoiced. You can have high productivity and low recovery — busy but not billing everything — or low productivity and high recovery — focused but underutilised. Watching both tells you where the problem is, not just that there is one.
Why does it matter to monitor both productivity and recovery?
Because they point to different fixes. Low productivity usually signals a workflow or management issue — too much admin, unclear priorities, or a team that needs better focus. Low recovery usually signals a billing issue — scope creep, write-offs, under-quoting or work that isn't making it onto invoices. Improving both by even a few percentage points compounds quickly through the capacity formula and can make a material difference to profitability. The Capacity Optimiser lets you test exactly this with your own numbers.
What's a good productivity or recovery rate for a service business?
It varies by industry and role, but as a general guide: productivity rates of 65–80% are typical for professional services, with new starters often at 40–60% while ramping up. Recovery rates of 75–90% are a common target — consistently below 70% usually points to a quoting, scope management or write-off issue worth investigating. In Magnifi, you can set productivity to ramp up month by month for new hires using Monthly Capacity, so the forecast reflects reality rather than assuming full capacity from day one.
If I change capacity inputs, does it update my income forecast?
Not automatically — and that distinction matters. Capacity is your team's potential to earn, not a confirmed income forecast. If you increase a sell rate or productivity %, your capacity figure changes but your income target only updates if you change it deliberately. What does flow through automatically is cost: add an employee and their salary hits the P&L immediately. Capacity vs Income then shows you the gap between what the new team can earn and what you're planning to earn — prompting the right conversation about whether to raise the income target.
How do I know if I need to hire?
Start with Capacity vs Income. A consistent shortfall across multiple months is the signal to look harder. But before concluding you need more headcount, check productivity and recovery first — a hire made before optimising those levers is an expensive fix for a problem that might not need one. If utilisation is already healthy and the shortfall persists, the FTE equivalent in Capacity vs Income translates the gap into a practical starting point for a hiring conversation. The Capacity Optimiser is a quick way to model the impact of an additional hire before setting up the full forecast.
What causes a capacity shortfall in a service business?
Usually one or more of five things: not enough headcount for the work volume; productivity below where it should be; recovery leaking billings; charge-out rates below market; or income targets that are too ambitious for the current team. Magnifi's capacity formula surfaces which lever is most likely the issue — because you can see exactly which input is dragging the capacity figure below the income target.
What's the difference between utilisation and recovery?
The terms are sometimes used interchangeably, but they refer to different things. Utilisation typically refers to the proportion of available time spent on any work — billable or not — and is closest to what Magnifi calls Productivity %. Recovery (or Efficiency %) specifically measures what proportion of productive time ends up on an invoice. In Magnifi, both are separate inputs precisely because they diagnose different problems.
Can I analyse capacity by team or service line?
Yes. The Teams feature lets you allocate both income and employee capacity across named teams by percentage, with each team showing its own surplus or shortfall. Employees can appear across multiple teams. For detailed cost reporting by group, use Divisions instead — Teams are designed purely for the capacity and income comparison.
Do I have to be a service business to use Magnifi?
No. Magnifi is tailored for service businesses where labour drives income — professional services, consulting, agencies, healthcare and similar. But businesses where income includes both labour and materials can still use it. Flag the relevant income accounts as Capacity Building in the Chart of Accounts, calculate an overall sell rate as total income divided by total billable hours, and the capacity framework holds for workforce planning either way.
Do I need Monthly Capacity or is Annual enough?
Annual Capacity is enough for most businesses — it gives a full-year view using consistent assumptions. Monthly Capacity is worth adding if your business is seasonal, if you have new hires ramping up, or if charge-out rates change during the year. You can start with Annual and add monthly detail at any time.
